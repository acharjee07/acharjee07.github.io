Research

Helping robots understand and act in changing environments

My research focuses on the connection between seeing, understanding, and acting. Collecting more data does not always resolve the uncertainty that matters for a task. I study how spatial representations, viewpoint selection, and feedback control can make robotic sensing purposeful.

Construction and infrastructure provide a demanding testbed: geometry changes, occlusions hide useful evidence, and a robot's camera placement limits what it can inspect. I approach these challenges through 3D perception, embodied sensing, and hands-on robot control, combining simulation with physical experiments.

The projects below explore three connected questions. For a concise catalog of all my work, visit All Projects.

Where should a robot look and move? How should it represent its surroundings? How can it reason about risk?

DeltaSeek: Embodiment-Aware Active Perception

Problem & platform

Construction environments change between visits. DeltaSeek studies how a robot's camera placement determines which changes it can observe, before deciding where to move next.

Husky A300 + UR5eROS 2GazeboRGB-DIFC

Methodology

  1. Convert a Revit IFC export into a room-scale benchmark with eight controlled changes.
  2. Evaluate visibility across 240 base poses: 60 positions at four yaw orientations.
  3. Combine each base pose with five arm postures for 1,200 wrist-camera configurations.
  4. Separate geometric observability from collision-aware base travel to an observation.

Results & limitations

The wrist camera observed two changes that were invisible from every chassis-camera pose. For mutually observable changes, median base travel was 6.0 m with wrist sensing and 15.2 m with chassis sensing.

These are geometric simulation results from one room and eight controlled changes. They do not establish robustness to sensor noise or physical-robot detection performance. Travel measures base translation, excluding arm motion and return travel.

LeRobot SO-101 Kinematics & Visual Servoing

From simulation to physical control

Built a control stack that converts image-space target error into joint commands for end-effector positioning. The same framework was validated in MuJoCo before deployment to a physical LeRobot SO-101.

Hardware demonstration from the project repository.

Differential IKJacobian ControlVisual ServoingMuJoCoLeRobotReal Hardware

Control pipeline

  1. Derive analytical forward kinematics and the symbolic Jacobian.
  2. Detect the visual target with OpenCV and compute image-space error.
  3. Use Jacobian-based differential inverse kinematics to generate joint velocities.
  4. Monitor singularities and apply predictive joint-limit handling.

The project demonstrates a closed-loop perception-to-control workflow with simulation and hardware implementations. The demo uses a visually tracked target; it is not a learned general-purpose manipulation policy.

Multimodal 3D Thermal Reconstruction

Building geometry with thermal context

Fused drone-captured RGB and thermal imagery into dense 3D building profiles for spatially localized inspection and energy diagnostics.

Reconstruction workflow

  1. Collect multimodal drone imagery of the building.
  2. Recover camera poses and sparse geometry with structure from motion in COLMAP.
  3. Map thermal information to sparse points and transfer it to a dense multi-view stereo reconstruction.
  4. Inspect spatially localized thermal patterns in the resulting point cloud.

This work provides a multimodal representation for inspection; automated defect detection and broader ground-truth evaluation are future directions.

Scene Graph-Guided Industrial Safety AI

From accident narratives to visual scenarios

Developed a pipeline that connects historical accident reports to structured hazard reasoning and visual safety scenarios using LLMs, scene graphs, diffusion models, and vision-language evaluation.

  1. Extract hazards and contextual relationships from accident narratives.
  2. Represent object-level spatial relationships as scene graphs.
  3. Generate visual scenarios guided by the structured representation.
  4. Evaluate semantic consistency between the intended hazard and the generated scene.

Public preprint: Scene Graph-Guided Generative AI Framework for Synthesizing and Evaluating Industrial Hazard Scenarios. arXiv:2511.13970, 2025.

The generated scenarios support research on safety training data. Synthetic scenarios require evaluation before use in a deployed hazard-detection system.
